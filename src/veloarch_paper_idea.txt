We propose a lightweight analytical framework for bottleneck-driven performance interpretability in neural network inference, which explains why a given model is slow on a target hardware architecture. Instead of only estimating aggregate latency or energy, our method decomposes execution at the layer level, attributing cost to compute and memory components and identifying the dominant constraint for each operation. By modeling the interaction between workload characteristics (e.g., operations, tensor sizes) and architectural parameters (e.g., parallelism, bandwidth), the system classifies layers as compute-bound or memory-bound and exposes fine-grained performance bottlenecks. This enables practitioners to diagnose inefficiencies, understand hardware–model mismatches, and guide optimization decisions without requiring expensive profiling or hardware-specific instrumentation.
